Mission Statement

MethaneSAT's data provides insights that can help reduce methane emissions from the oil and gas industry

✓MethaneSAT · Our Mission Page — https://www.methanesat.org/mission
Problems Solved

Small, widespread sources account for at least 70% of methane emissions from oil and gas production. MethaneSAT sees what others don't and provides the high-resolution data necessary to measure whether action is taken.

✓MethaneSAT · Solving a crucial climate challenge — https://www.methanesat.org/
Stated Goals

MethaneSAT data provides energy producers, government agencies and other users access to insights to help identify the greatest opportunities for reducing methane emissions.

✓MethaneSAT · Our Mission Page — https://www.methanesat.org/mission
Stated Goals

While operational, MethaneSAT monitored areas that produce 80% or more of the world's oil and gas. It measured emissions across very large areas in a matter of seconds and saw changes in concentrations as small as 3 parts per billion.

✓MethaneSAT · Our Mission Page — https://www.methanesat.org/mission
Target Audience

Data from MethaneSAT will be freely available to anyone with an internet connection, making it possible to see where emissions are coming from with unparalleled precision, making it easier for both operators and regulators to find and fix problems faster

Partners

In addition to parent organization EDF, MethaneSAT partners include Harvard University's School of Engineering and Applied Sciences, the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory, and the New Zealand Space Agency. The combined mission team has over 70 experts worldwide with experience in spaceflight, remote sensing, and data analytics. The satellite was built in Colorado by the Space & Mission Systems unit of BAE Systems, Inc. (formerly Ball Aerospace) and Blue Canyon Technologies.
